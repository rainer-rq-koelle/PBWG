ASMA/DSMA development

Problem Space

By tradition we are using a “histogram” based approach to identify entry sectors for ASMA.
This approach shall be also applied to DSMA, i.e., identifying respective exit sectors.

Using “sectors” offers flexibility as changes to the operational environment and airspace organisation will undergo changes. Entry/exit fixes or waypoints may be moved, introduced/removed.
Experience within the European airspace has demonstrated that such “system point” changes occur regularly making it impossible to maintain a respective database (in sync with AIRAC cycles).
Tactical sequencing and selective use of nuances in the clearance limit, make flights pass abeam such waypoints.

Sector identification is based on the crossing of the respective approximation of the arrival/depature airspace.
By definition we use concentric circles of radius 40NM and 100NM around the aerodrome reference point (ARP).
Note: the vertical flight efficiency (VFE) horizon is 200NM. With a view to study XSMA/VFE, we may need to extend the ASMA/DSMA = XSMA references to also include 200NM ranges.
Conceptually, the algorithm should be paramterised, if future studies would require/identify another radius.

The visual sector identification aims at identifying up to about 5-6 “sectors” unless there is operational evidence that the operations can be better described by using additional sectors.

The XSMA algorithm determines a reference time for similar flight operations.
Similar flight operations are characterised by the * respective entry/exit sector; * aircraft velocity range as a combination of weight turbulence category and propulsion system, i.e. L/M/H & P/T/J (e.g. Light Piston LP, Medium Turboprop MT, Heavy Jet HJ), while the A380 and a Antonov are typically associated as “Super” heavy ‘S’ from a WTC perspective, these aircraft as grouped together with HJ. * runway used for landing/ASMA or depature/DSMA.

Discussion are on-going about adapting the newer groupings based on the recent recatorigation RECAT of aircraft in a pair-to-pair relationship to optimise the pairwise WTC separation.
This is currently out of scope, but from a conceptual perspective it should not influence the sectorisation identification. The newer RECAT labels being a further breakdown of the existing WTC.
It may however influence sample sizes required for the determination of the reference times.

The sample size for the determination of reference times is currently set as n = 20, given that the reference period covers movement within a full year.

Approach

The XSMA analysis builds on the establishment of a reference time sample for the respective study airport.

For this purpose we work with the 40NM and 100NM milestones embedded in the APDF data set. The APDF data set also provides for the bearing from the aerodrome reference point to the flight milestone, i.e., we do not have to calculate this from the ARP.

Reference Period Governance

Each study establishes an agreed start reference set for its selected reference period. The 2024 set developed here is therefore specific to the current study; other studies may agree a different reference period. Once established, the study reference remains in use for subsequent analysis batches until an operational change or a new study decision requires a revised sectorisation and reference set.

Full Study-Airport ARR Review

The same proposal workflow is now applied to the complete 13-airport 2024 ARR study set. The central APDF archive provides 11 extracts; the LGAV and LPPT extracts are read from their supplementary APDF archives. The airport-specific source map below makes these inputs explicit, so the complete study set can be reproduced without revisiting source-data discovery.

The first-round candidates remain a proposal-for-approval, not a final sector definition. The second-round merge screen makes a transparent recommendation for adjacent candidate basins when the valley is not clearly separating flows and their conditional weighted median difference is no more than 80 seconds. This is an analyst-support rule: its recommendations do not replace the operational decision to retain or remove a boundary.

Merge Evidence

  • Initial cuts identify local geometry; they are not automatic final sectors.
  • The provisional merge screen requires a non-separating valley and conditional weighted P50 difference of no more than 80 seconds.
  • For every possible merge, we now calculate the actual effect of pooling both sides on their runway/class P20 references.
  • The evidence records the movement-weighted P20 shift, the share of movements shifted by more than 80 seconds, and the largest individual runway/class shift. These values support, but do not replace, the analyst decision.
  • A future merge threshold should be based on these pooled effects and checked against operational knowledge, rather than inferred from one visual pattern.

Early Cross-Airport Check

  • At EDDM, the three suggested merge interiors have weighted pooled P20 shifts of approximately 7–30 seconds, with no more than 0.2% of movements shifted by over 80 seconds. This is supportive evidence for those recommendations.
  • At EGKK, the 40 NM suggested merge has a weighted pooled P20 shift of about 12 seconds and affects 0.2% of movements beyond 80 seconds. In contrast, the two retained 100 NM cuts affect approximately 13% and 28% of movements beyond 80 seconds when pooled, supporting their retention.
  • The maximum individual runway/class shift may still be large where a cell is sparse. It is therefore a useful audit flag, but should not become a stand- alone acceptance threshold.

EDDF ARR Candidate Sector Review

EDDF 40 NM shoulder at 131 degrees. The 131-degree candidate lies between a strong peak near 111 degrees and a weaker shoulder near 151 degrees. The shoulder weakens markedly at broader smoothing, which is consistent with tactical spreading of arrival trajectories. However, its conditional weighted median differs by about 105 seconds, and pooling the two sides would change the P20 reference by more than 80 seconds for 6.2% of the valid movements, including high-support runway/class cells. The sectorisation therefore retains the 131-degree boundary: it preserves meaningful timing variation that may reflect runway alignment and direct crosswind/final shortcuts, rather than treating the visual shoulder as harmless noise.

Decision: All boundaries retained. 6 sectors at 40 NM (min width 25°), 6 sectors at 100 NM (min width 29°). ✅ APPROVED

Departure Sectorization (DSMA): EDDF Development Example

The same valley-based algorithm validated for arrivals (ASMA) applies to departures (DSMA). Departures cross the terminal airspace boundary outbound, with bearing measured at the 40 NM and 100 NM range rings. The observed transit time runs from takeoff to the range crossing; additional time is only calculated once a reference has been joined.

Methodology Consistency

Algorithm parameters (identical to ARR): - Smoothing bandwidth: 6 degrees (circular Gaussian) - Minimum relative prominence: 0.02 (2%) - Valley safety fraction: 0.25 - Timing threshold: 80 seconds

Expected differences: - Departure flows may show more variability due to SID routing and vectoring - Peaks may be less prominent than arrivals due to tactical routing - Sector widths may differ based on departure corridor structure

Departure Sectorization (DSMA)

The DSMA review applies the same circular, valley-based workflow to DEP movements in the 2024 APDF reference set. Bearing is observed at the outbound 40 NM and 100 NM crossings. The resulting sector definitions will later be used with runway and aircraft-class samples to build the DSMA reference times; the figures below deliberately assess traffic geometry first.

Review Settings

  • Central circular Gaussian smoothing bandwidth: 6 degrees.
  • Sensitivity check: 4, 6, and 8 degree bandwidths.
  • Substantial peak/minimum screen: 2 percent relative prominence.
  • Candidate cuts: valley-safe rounded to 5 degrees.
  • Merge review: valley/lower-peak ratio at least 0.25 and conditional weighted P50 difference no more than 80 seconds.

Evidence Guidance

The first-round proposal now adds a conservative three-tier evidence guide. A cut is marked Propose merge only when it is a shallow valley and both the conditional weighted P50 difference (at most 80 seconds) and the pooling impact (weighted P20 shift at most 30 seconds; at most 1 percent of movements above 80 seconds) are small. A Propose retain flag identifies a deep valley or material timing/pooling effect. All mixed cases remain Analyst review: the guide reduces routine decisions, but does not replace operational knowledge. North overrun is determined independently for each airport and range.
